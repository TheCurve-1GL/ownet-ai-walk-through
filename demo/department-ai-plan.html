Why most AI pilots stall

They start with a tool and go looking for a problem

The work gets added on top of people's day jobs, so they end up under more pressure, not less. Nobody measures what changed, and the savings never reach the bottom line.

The Department AI Plan starts the other way round. We look across the department's main processes, sort the regular work in each, and only then decide where AI fits and where it doesn't. It's wider and lighter than a Deep Dive, so you see the whole picture quickly.

Where we usually start

Back-office teams with lots of routine, rules-based work show the biggest share of time freed, need the least checking and are the easiest to measure.

  • Finance
  • Procurement and contracts
  • Commercial teams
  • People and HR
  • IT and digital
How we sort the work

Every task goes into one of four types

The same four types drive the estimator below and the plan you get at the end.

1

Keep it human

Judgement, relationships and decisions that need a person. AI stays out.

2

AI drafts, a person checks

AI does the first pass. A person reviews it and owns the result.

3

AI handles it

Routine, rules-based work that AI runs, with a person picking up the exceptions.

4

New with AI

Work you couldn't afford to do before, plus the oversight that keeps AI honest.

How it works

Eight weeks, one department

Weeks 1 to 2

Pick the department and list its processes

A half-day kick-off with you and the department head. We agree the main processes to cover, start from Ownet's process model and talk to the people who do the work.

Weeks 3 to 4

Sort the work in each process

Each regular task goes into Type 1, 2, 3 or 4. Where we can, we time a few tasks with and without AI rather than guess.

Weeks 5 to 6

Put numbers on it

Hours and cost a month for each change, the one-off cost to set it up, the AI running costs, software you could drop and the risks you'd reduce.

Weeks 7 to 8

The 90-day plan

We walk you and your team through the findings, test them together, then hand over the final plan.

After the plan

From plan to working change

Pilot build and handover. Ownet sets up the AI tools, updates the process model and trains your team, at a fixed price quoted from the plan.

Go deeper where it pays. An AI Process Deep Dive takes the top process from the plan and gets it ready to build.

Then department by department. Each department you add follows the same eight weeks, building on the same process model.

Questions

Straight answers

Is the estimate a quote or a promise?

No. It's an illustrative estimate from the assumptions shown, to help you decide if a closer look is worth it. The eight-week plan replaces it with your own figures.

We already have Copilot and IT is on it.

Good, that's a head start. Tools are only half of it. The plan looks at the work itself, department by department, so the licences you already pay for actually take time off people's desks.

What will it cost to put in place?

That's one of the things the plan tells you. Every change comes with its one-off set-up cost and its running cost, so you can see how quickly each one pays back before you commit. The quickest wins often use software you already pay for.

Procurement will take six months.

It doesn't have to. The plan can go on an existing Ownet order, so there's no new supplier to set up.

Does this mean job cuts?

That isn't the aim. The plan is built to move people onto higher-value work, slow hiring or cut overtime. What you do with the freed time is your decision.

Which one do I need?

One process in depth, or a whole department?

Many clients do both: the Department AI Plan finds where the time goes, then a Deep Dive gets the top process ready to build.
