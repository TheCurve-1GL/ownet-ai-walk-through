The problem

AI spend is rising faster than AI results

Most organisations already have AI in the building, from Copilot licences to tools staff have found for themselves. What's usually missing is a clear picture of where it's being used, whether it's working and what's holding it back.

Without that picture, investment goes to the loudest idea rather than the best one, and the same blockers, such as scattered data, undocumented processes and unclear rules, stall every project in turn.

This is for you if

  • Your board is asking for an AI strategy and you need a fact base
  • You've run pilots that didn't scale and want to know why
  • Staff are using AI tools nobody approved
  • You want to know which departments to start with
What we look at

Five areas, scored and compared

Processes

How settled and documented your processes are. Clear, repeatable work is quick to improve; messy work needs sorting out first.

Data

Whether the data AI needs is in one place, trusted and allowed to be used.

People and culture

Skills, appetite and trust. Freed time is only real if people use the tools.

Technology

The systems and licences you already pay for, and what they could already do.

Rules and leadership

Security, regulation, governance and sponsorship from the top.

Current AI use

A short, no-blame check of how staff use AI today, including tools nobody approved.

How it works

Four steps to a clear picture

Step 1

Kick-off

We agree scope with your sponsor and pick the departments to look at.

Step 2

Gather

Short interviews, a staff AI use survey and a review of your processes, data and systems.

Step 3

Score

Each area gets a maturity score from 1 to 5, with the evidence behind it.

Step 4

Recommend

A ranked list of AI opportunities and a roadmap of what to fix first.

What you get

Clear findings you can act on

  • A maturity score for each of the five areas, with the evidence
  • Your top 10 AI opportunities, ranked by value and ease
  • The blockers to fix first, and who should own them
  • A short board paper in plain English
  • A recommendation on which process to take into a Department AI Plan

The five maturity levels

  • 1 Watching. The board is asking, but there's no approved tool.
  • 2 Personal use. Staff use their own tools, often without saying.
  • 3 Tools rolled out. Approved tools and training, but the work looks the same.
  • 4 Work redesigned. Some processes rebuilt around AI and measured.
  • 5 Run on AI. Most work redesigned and the benefits banked.

Most large organisations are at level 2 or 3.

Questions

Straight answers

How long does it take?

Typically four to six weeks, depending on how many departments are in scope.

How much of our people's time will it need?

A kick-off with your sponsor, short interviews with a handful of people in each department and a ten-minute staff survey.

What happens afterwards?

Most clients take their top opportunity straight into a Department AI Plan, so the findings turn into a costed plan within weeks.
