The problem

AI is moving faster than the rules around it

Staff are using AI to draft, summarise and decide, often with tools nobody approved. Meanwhile regulators are setting expectations, from the EU AI Act to sector rules in financial services, and boards want to know who is accountable when AI gets it wrong.

Banning AI doesn't work and pushes it out of sight. The answer is clear, proportionate controls that people can follow without slowing the work down.

This is for you if

  • You don't know where AI is being used across the organisation
  • Your AI policy is a page of don'ts nobody reads
  • Internal audit or a regulator has asked how you control AI
  • You want to scale AI without adding new risks

Questions

Straight answers

Does the EU AI Act apply to us?

It can apply to UK organisations that sell into the EU or whose AI affects people there. We map which of your AI uses it touches and what that means in practice.

Will this slow AI down?

It shouldn't. Low-risk uses get light-touch rules so people can get on. Tighter controls are kept for the uses where something serious could go wrong.

Can you work with our existing risk team?

Yes, and we'd recommend it. We build AI into the framework your risk and audit teams already run, so there's one way of managing risk, not two.
